How to Make a Family Screen-Time Agreement Kids Will Actually Follow

Screen-time battles usually come from rules that feel random to kids. A written agreement you make together turns "because I said so" into "this is what we decided."

Step 1: Talk before you write

Ask your kids what they love about their screens and what bugs them. Share your own worries calmly. Kids are more likely to follow rules they helped make.

Step 2: Decide when screens are OK

Many families use "after" rules: after homework, after chores, after playing outside. It is easier than counting minutes.

Step 3: Pick screen-free places and times

Common ones: meals, the car on short trips, bedrooms, and the hour before bed. Grown-ups follow these too.

Step 4: Agree on what happens if the rules are broken

Keep it simple and predictable, like losing screens the next day. Decide it ahead of time so it is not a fight in the moment.

Step 5: Sign it and post it

Everyone signs, including parents. Put it on the fridge and review it every few months as kids grow.

Have a boredom list ready

Write down screen-free ideas with your kids: Lego, drawing, reading, outside games. When they say "I'm bored," point to the list.
