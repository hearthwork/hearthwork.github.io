Why a Christmas countdown helps

Kids already feel Christmas coming. A countdown gives that waiting a job. One circle to color, one link to tear, or one ornament to mark turns "how many days?" into something they can see and do. It also gives the family a shared daily moment that does not need a big activity every night.

December 1 to 25 vs Advent

A Christmas countdown calendar usually runs December 1 to 25, because that is what kids search for and what most printable calendars use. Liturgical Advent is different. In 2026, Advent starts on Sunday, November 29 and runs to Christmas Eve, December 24. That is 26 nights of waiting focused on getting ready for the birth of Jesus.

You can keep both. Use a Dec 1 to 25 countdown for the kid-friendly mark on the wall, and keep a short Advent rhythm (candles, a reading, a prayer) for the evenings. Our Advent Activities for Kids guide covers the Advent side. This post focuses on the Christmas countdown calendar.

How to run a simple countdown at home

  1. Pick one format: color-in calendar, tree ornaments, or paper chain. One is enough.
  2. Hang it at kid height on the fridge, a door, or by the tree.
  3. Mark it at the same time every day. Right after dinner works well.
  4. Keep the mark short. Color, sticker, or tear. Then stop.
  5. Add one small activity a few times a week, not every night.
  6. Missed a day? Mark today and keep going. No catching up.

Color-in, tree, and paper chain options

Color-in calendar. Twenty-five numbered circles for December 1 to 25. Kids color one a day. It is the simplest option for ages 2 to 5.

Christmas tree countdown. Numbered ornament circles on a page you hang by the tree. Color or sticker one ornament each day. Good when you want the countdown to feel like decorating.

Paper chain. Twenty-five links to cut, color, and loop. Tear one off each night. Kids love the sound and the shrinking chain.

You do not need all three. Print the pages you will use and leave the rest for next year.

25 simple Christmas countdown activity ideas

  • Color today's countdown circle or tear one chain link
  • Add one ornament to the tree
  • Read a Christmas picture book
  • Sing one carol at bedtime
  • Make hot cocoa together
  • Cut paper snowflakes for a window
  • Write a card to a grandparent
  • Bake one tray of cookies
  • Drive or walk to see Christmas lights
  • Act out the nativity story
  • Make a small gift for a sibling
  • Wrap one present together
  • Give away one outgrown toy or coat
  • Eat dinner by candlelight
  • String a popcorn garland
  • Fill a bag for the food bank
  • Build a blanket fort and read inside it
  • Pray for one friend by name
  • Learn one carol by heart as a family
  • Set out the nativity, one piece at a time

Save the bigger ones (baking, lights, food bank) for weekends. Weeknights only need the daily mark plus one short extra when you have room.

Countdown tips by age

Ages 2 to 5: big circles, stickers, and tearing the chain. Keep it under a minute and let them do the mark themselves.

Ages 6 to 8: let them lead the daily mark and pick the activity from a short list you already chose.

Ages 9 to 12: hand them the week planner. They choose the countdown moment and one giving idea for the week.
