Morning and Bedtime Routine Charts for Kids (Free Printable)

Bottom line: a short routine chart on the wall, 4 to 6 steps long, does the reminding for you. Kids follow the chart, you stop nagging, and mornings and nights get calmer.

Why routine charts work

Young kids do better when they know what comes next. A chart turns "what do I do now?" into something they can check themselves. It also gives you a neutral thing to point at instead of repeating the same words ten times.

Morning routine steps by age

Ages 2 to 5: potty, get dressed with help, eat breakfast, brush teeth, shoes on.

Ages 6 to 8: make bed, get dressed, breakfast, brush teeth and hair, pack school bag, shoes and coat.

Ages 9 to 12: get up with an alarm, dress, breakfast, check planner, pack lunch and bag, out the door on time.

Bedtime routine steps by age

Ages 2 to 5: bath, pajamas, brush teeth, two books, hug and lights out.

Ages 6 to 8: tidy room, shower, pajamas, brush teeth, lay out tomorrow's clothes, read for 15 minutes.

Ages 9 to 12: screens off and charging outside the bedroom, shower, prep for tomorrow, read, lights out.

How to set it up

  1. Pick 4 to 6 steps together. Fewer is better.
  2. Write them in order on the chart. For kids who cannot read yet, draw a small picture next to each step.
  3. Post it at kid height where the routine happens: bedroom door, bathroom, or by the front door.
  4. Practice for one week. Walk through it with them, then step back.
  5. Track it. A check or sticker each day builds a streak, and a small family reward makes it fun.

Keep it working

If a step keeps getting skipped, move it earlier or break it into smaller steps. Review the chart every few months as kids grow.
