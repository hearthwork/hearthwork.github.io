Chore Chart for 4 Year Olds (Free Printable With Pictures)

Bottom line: a 4 year old can handle 2 to 4 short chores a day, and a picture chore chart is the best way to make them stick. Keep every job under three minutes, stay in the room while they work, and teach with pictures instead of a written list.

What chores can a 4 year old do?

Fewer and shorter than a 5 year old. At 4, kids can follow a one or two step job with you nearby. They like to help, but they still need the grown-up in the room. The goal is the habit and the help, not a perfect result.

Good chores for 4 year olds:

  • Bedroom and toys: put toys in the bin, clothes in the hamper, shoes by the door, put books on the shelf, help pull up the covers.
  • Kitchen (with help): clear your own plate, wipe the table, set the forks, carry napkins, wash fruit, stir when cooking.
  • Around the house: hang the coat, sort socks into pairs, dust a low shelf with a sock, sweep crumbs with a small broom.
  • Pets and plants (with a grown-up): feed the pet, fill the water bowl, water one plant.
  • Outside (with a grown-up): gather sticks, pull a few weeds, rinse the car with a hose.

Save harder jobs like emptying the dishwasher alone, folding laundry, or sweeping a whole room for age 5 and up. For a full list from preschool to age 12, see our guide to age-appropriate chores for kids 3 to 12. When your child is ready for a longer list, try our chore chart for 5 year olds.

How many chores should a 4 year old have?

Two to four a day is plenty. One in the morning and one or two after dinner works for most preschoolers. Each chore should take under three minutes. If the whole list takes longer than 10 minutes, cut it. A short chart that gets used beats a long one that gets ignored.

Why a picture chore chart for preschoolers

Most 4 year olds cannot read a written list. A picture chore chart shows the next job so your child can see it and start without waiting for you to read it out loud. You point to the picture instead of repeating yourself. Checking the box or adding a sticker is a small reward that keeps the chart interesting.

How to set up a chore chart for a 4 year old

  1. Pick 2 to 4 chores together. Let your child choose one of them. Kids care more about a job they picked.
  2. Attach each chore to a time. Toys before bath. Plate right after dinner. Shoes right after coming inside. A chore with a set time needs fewer reminders.
  3. Teach side by side. Do the job with them for a week or two before you expect any of it alone. At 4, "alone" still means you are in the room.
  4. Post the chart at kid height. The fridge, the back of the bedroom door, or next to the toy bin.
  5. Check it together before bed. A quick look and a high five for what got done matters more than any prize.

Sample chore chart for a 4 year old

Morning: put toys away (or make the bed with help), clothes in the hamper.

After coming inside: shoes by the door.

After dinner: clear my plate, put books away.

With a grown-up, a few times a week: feed the pet, water one plant.

Keep the morning list to one or two boxes. Preschool mornings are short, and getting dressed already takes work.

Should you pay a 4 year old for chores?

No. Everyday family chores stay unpaid at this age. A 4 year old clears a plate because they live here, not for a coin. Stickers, a high five, or choosing a story at bedtime are enough. If you want to start money lessons later, wait until closer to age 5 or 6 and keep allowance separate from the chart. Our allowance chart by age covers how much and how when you get there.

Common mistakes

  • Too many chores. Six boxes a day is a wall for a preschooler. Start with two.
  • Expecting solo work too soon. At 4, you stay close. Solo comes later.
  • Redoing their work in front of them. Toys in the bin counts. Fix the lid later, or not at all.
  • A written list with no pictures. If they cannot read it, the chart does not help.
  • Chores with no set time. Do it sometime today turns into a fight at bedtime.
