Chore Chart for 5 Year Olds (Free Printable With Pictures)

Bottom line: a 5 year old can handle 3 to 5 real chores a day, and a picture chore chart is the easiest way to make them stick. Pick chores that take under five minutes, teach each one with your child before you expect it alone, and post the chart at their eye level.

What chores can a 5 year old do?

More than most parents expect. At 5, kids can follow a two or three step job, they like to be trusted, and they want to help. They are also slow and messy at it. That is fine. The goal at this age is the habit, not a perfect result.

Good chores for 5 year olds:

  • Bedroom: make the bed (pull up the covers), put dirty clothes in the hamper, put clean clothes in a drawer, tidy toys into bins.
  • Kitchen: set the table, clear your own plate, wipe the table, put spoons away from the dishwasher, wash fruit, help measure and stir.
  • Around the house: put shoes and coat away, hang up the backpack, sort recycling, empty small trash cans, dust with a sock on your hand.
  • Pets and plants: feed the pet, fill the water bowl, water the plants.
  • Outside: gather sticks, pull weeds with a grown-up, rake a small pile of leaves, help wash the car.

How many chores should a 5 year old have?

Three to five a day is plenty. A simple split is two in the morning and two after dinner. Each one should take under five minutes. If the whole list takes longer than 15 minutes, it is too long for a 5 year old, and the chart will stop working within a week.

Why a chore chart with pictures

Most 5 year olds are just starting to read, so a written list means a grown-up has to read it out loud every time. A picture chore chart lets your child see the next job and do it on their own. You point to the picture instead of giving the reminder, and the chart becomes the one asking. Checking the box or adding a sticker is its own small reward.

How to set up a chore chart for a 5 year old

  1. Pick 3 to 5 chores together. Let your child choose one of them. Kids care more about a job they picked.
  2. Attach each chore to a time. Bed right after getting dressed. Plate right after dinner. Toys before bath. A chore with a set time does not need a reminder.
  3. Teach one chore at a time. Do it for them, then do it together, then watch them do it, then let them do it alone. This takes about a week per chore.
  4. Post the chart at kid height. The fridge, the back of the bedroom door, or next to the hamper.
  5. Check it together at the end of the day. A quick look at the chart before bed, with a high five for what got done, matters more than any prize.

Sample chore chart for a 5 year old

Morning: make my bed, clothes in the hamper.

After school or before dinner: tidy my toys, set the table.

After dinner: clear my plate.

Once a week: water the plants, help put groceries away.

Swap in feeding the pet if you have one. It is the chore most 5 year olds are proudest of.

Should you pay a 5 year old for chores?

Keep everyday family chores unpaid. A 5 year old clears their plate because they live here, not for a quarter. If you want to start money lessons, a small weekly allowance in clear jars works well at this age, separate from the chart. Our allowance chart by age covers how much and how. For rewards, keep them small and tied to a whole week, like choosing Friday dinner or a trip to the park.

Common mistakes

  • Too many chores. Ten boxes a day is a wall. Start with three.
  • Redoing their work in front of them. A lumpy bed is a made bed. Fix it later, or not at all.
  • Chores with no set time. Do it sometime today turns into a fight at bedtime.
  • A chart that never gets checked. If nobody looks at it, it is wallpaper by week two.
  • Changing the list every few days. Keep the same chores for a month, then add one.
