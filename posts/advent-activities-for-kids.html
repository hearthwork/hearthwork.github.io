Advent Activities for Kids (Free Printable Checklist)

Bottom line: pick a short nightly Advent rhythm you can keep, then add two or three simple activities a week. Advent 2026 starts on Sunday, November 29 and runs to Christmas Eve, Thursday, December 24. That is 26 nights, and you do not need to fill every one.

What Advent is, in kid terms

Advent is the four Sundays and the weeks before Christmas. It is a season of waiting and getting ready. Kids already know how to wait for Christmas. Advent gives that waiting a shape, so the weeks point toward the birth of Jesus instead of toward a pile of presents.

A 10-minute nightly Advent rhythm

  1. Gather at the same time each night. Right after dinner, still at the table, works well.
  2. Light the candles for the week. One the first week, two the second, and so on.
  3. Read one short passage, like Isaiah 9:2-7 or Luke 1:26-38. For little ones, tell it in your own words.
  4. Ask one question. Who is waiting in this story? What did God promise?
  5. Pray, sing one verse of a carol, and tear one link off the paper chain.

That is the whole thing. The activities below are extras for the nights and weekends you have room.

24 simple Advent activities for kids

  • Set up the nativity together and move one piece closer each day.
  • Make a paper chain countdown with one link per night.
  • Bake cookies and take a plate to a neighbor.
  • Read a Christmas picture book by candlelight.
  • Cut paper snowflakes and tape them to the windows.
  • Drive or walk to see Christmas lights with cocoa.
  • Sing one carol at bedtime.
  • Make salt dough ornaments.
  • Act out the nativity story with bathrobes and towels.
  • Build a gingerbread or graham cracker house.
  • Write cards to grandparents.
  • Cut paper stars for a window.
  • String a popcorn garland.
  • Give away outgrown toys, coats, and books.
  • Eat dinner by candlelight.
  • Make a small gift for a sibling.
  • Wrap presents together.
  • Go to a carol service or a lessons and carols night.
  • Make orange and clove pomanders.
  • Fill a grocery bag for the food bank.
  • Put on a carol playlist and make hot cocoa.
  • Build a blanket fort and read inside it.
  • Pray for one friend each night.
  • Learn one carol by heart as a family.

Advent activities by age

Ages 2 to 5: keep it hands-on. Let them move a nativity piece, tear the chain link, color a paper star, and stir the cookie dough. Short songs like Away in a Manger stick fast.

Ages 6 to 8: they love a job. Let them cut snowflakes, play a part in the nativity, write cards, and choose toys to give away. Find Bethlehem on a map or globe together.

Ages 9 to 12: hand them real responsibility. They can read the evening passage aloud, plan and bake a neighbor gift with little help, and lead the family prayer one night a week.

Make it work on busy weeknights

  • Put the week's activities on the calendar on Sunday night.
  • Save the bigger ones, like baking or a lights drive, for Friday and Saturday.
  • Keep a box with candles, matches, the chain, and the book in one spot.
  • Missed a night? Skip it and pick up with today. No catching up.

Giving and serving

Advent is a good season to give something away together. Fill a bag for the food bank, shovel or rake for an older neighbor, or let kids pick a family to give to through your church. Let kids help choose, carry, and hand it over. That is the part they remember.
